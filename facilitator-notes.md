## Why the format changed from 2025

Last year's curriculum walked all three tracks through building one fixed reference app step-by-step. It was hard to follow — at a recent Build Night, only 2–3 of ~20 attendees could work through comparable material. Meanwhile the standout moment was a first-timer pointed at the right tool who built her first app in an hour ("I did more in an hour than in three years"). This year optimizes for that: match people to the right tool fast, then coach.

Related: attendance held all weekend last year; the gap was team *completion* (~30 started, 17 finished). Everything we do should push toward small scope and finishing.

## Coaching model (Sept 19–20)

- Most teams need nothing. A few need a lot. Float, don't hover.
- Triage order: (1) can't get started at all — sit with them until unstuck; (2) blocked by a bug/tool issue — timebox and fix; (3) "make it better" asks — pointers only.
- Recurring rescue moves: re-scoping an overambitious idea; the "ask chat to write the build prompt" handoff; credit budgeting; laptop/environment triage.
- Never take the keyboard for more than a demonstration moment. They build it, you unblock it.

## Tone

- No stack shaming. Any tool is legitimate, including "just ChatGPT and a deck."
- The floor matters more than the ceiling: a Tier 1 participant finishing *anything* is a bigger win than a Tier 3 team adding one more feature.
- Judging cares about the pillar. When coaching, keep asking "how does this serve the pillar you picked?"
