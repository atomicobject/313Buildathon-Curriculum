# Tier 1: Intro to AI Agents

**Audience:** Participants who have never (or barely) used an AI assistant. No coding experience assumed, and none required to participate in the buildathon.

**Length:** 30 minutes of content + 10–15 minutes Q&A

**Format:** Live demo-driven. Slides optional; one big screen with a chat assistant open is the core visual.

## Session goal

By the end, every attendee can hold a productive working conversation with an AI assistant and knows what a winnable buildathon project looks like *without writing code*. A well-developed concept with a pitch deck and mockups, grounded in one of the city's pillars, is a legitimate submission.

## Outline

### 1. What an AI assistant actually is (5 min)

- It's a conversation partner, not a search engine. You go back and forth; it remembers the conversation.
- You don't need special commands or magic words. Talk to it like a sharp collaborator who knows a lot but knows nothing about *you* until you tell it.
- Name the tools they'll have access to this weekend (see `../resources/tools-and-credits.md`) and show where to sign in.

### 2. Live demo: develop a buildathon idea in conversation (12 min)

Do this live, unscripted, with a pillar-relevant example. Beat-by-beat:

1. Start vague on purpose: *"I live in Detroit and I think neighbors have a hard time finding out about city services. I want to do something about that for a hackathon."*
2. Show how to give context: paste in the pillar description, describe who you're building for.
3. Ask it to push back: *"What's weak about this idea? What already exists?"*
4. Narrow to something buildable in a weekend.
5. Ask for concrete outputs: a one-paragraph pitch, a list of key screens, an outline for a 3-minute demo presentation.

Teaching points to hit during the demo: iterate instead of accepting the first answer; be specific about what you want back; you can always say "no, more like this."

### 3. What you can produce without code (8 min)

- **A pitch deck:** ask the assistant to draft slides, then refine them together.
- **Mockups / a simple site:** show one no-code path (e.g., ChatGPT's sites feature) turning the idea into something visual and clickable.
- **A plan:** user stories, a rollout plan, a budget sketch — judges care that you understood the problem and the pillar.
- Set expectations honestly: you may not ship a working app this weekend, and that's fine. Solving the *problem* well matters more than the tech.

### 4. Your weekend playbook (5 min)

- Step 1: pick your pillar and write down who you're helping.
- Step 2: develop the idea in conversation (like the demo).
- Step 3: produce artifacts — pitch, mockups, demo outline.
- Step 4: when you're stuck or curious about going further, grab a coach. If you want to try making a real app, the Tier 2 material (`tier-2-build-with-ai-tools.md`) shows the tools — coaches can get you started.

### Q&A (10–15 min)

Common questions to be ready for: "Do I need to pay for these tools?" (no — credits/free tiers, see resource sheet), "Can the AI build the whole thing for me?" (it can build a lot, but *your* understanding of the problem is what gets judged), "What if I run out of credits?" (ask a coach before burning through them).

## Facilitator notes

- Keep the demo messy and real. Recovering from a bad AI answer live is the most valuable thing they'll see.
- Watch for attendees who clearly belong in Tier 2 — invite them to the next Tier 2 slot.
- The win condition for this session: nobody leaves thinking "I can't participate because I can't code."
